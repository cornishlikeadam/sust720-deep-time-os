## 1. The Four Stakeholder Positions

```
                     [ WHOSE WORK CHANGES? ]
                                │
       ┌────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
       ▼                        ▼                        ▼                        ▼
  [ 1. USERS ]          [ 2. DECIDERS ]          [ 3. AFFECTED ]          [ 4. BURDENED ]
 Graduate Students       SCAD Faculty             Indigenous Elders        Data Annotators
 Peer Collaborators      Program Director         Sustainability Clients   Data Center Towns
 Studio Tech Leads       Accreditation Board      Future Alumni            7th Gen Humans & Rivers
```

### Position 1: Users (Those Directly Interacting with the Tool)
* **Primary Actors:** SCAD Graduate Sustainable Design Students (M.A. / M.F.A.), Student Design Teams, Studio Facilitators.
* **Interests:** Rapidly exploring plausible 500-year futures; elevating regenerative system design craft; avoiding clichéd dystopian worldbuilding; graduating with portfolio-grade anti-fragile sustainability blueprints.
* **Risks:** Creative atrophy; cognitive over-reliance on synthetic outputs; loss of foundational research skills; erosion of personal aesthetic voice.
* **Benefits:** Ability to visualize complex systems; rapid multi-scenario stress-testing; breaking through writer's block during regenerative system blueprinting.
* **Concerns:** Feeling that the AI is "doing the real imagining"; fear of IP infringement; uncertainty regarding SCAD academic integrity policies.
* **Unresolved Questions:** Does using AI accelerate worldbuilding at the expense of students' internal conceptual understanding?

### Position 2: Decision Makers (Those with Authority to Permit, Mandate, or Grade)
* **Primary Actors:** SUST 720 Course Faculty, SCAD Design for Sustainability Department Chair, Academic Integrity Council, NASAD Accreditation Evaluators.
* **Interests:** Maintaining SCAD’s reputation for rigorous design excellence; ensuring students meet graduate learning outcomes; fostering critical literacy around AI.
* **Risks:** Grading synthetic regurgitation rather than authentic student ability; accreditation scrutiny; legal liabilities over copyrighted training sets.
* **Benefits:** Higher analytical rigor in multi-century system resilience; students better prepared for leadership roles in regenerative infrastructure and long-range foresight.
* **Concerns:** Faculty burnout trying to detect AI misuse; difficulty assessing authentic intellectual contribution in collaborative human-AI work.
* **Unresolved Questions:** How do faculty meaningfully assess the *process* of deep-time contemplation if the artifact is polished by algorithms?

### Position 3: People Affected (Those Impacted by Outcomes Without Direct Input)
* **Primary Actors:** Indigenous Knowledge Keepers & Elders whose epistemologies are engaged; Future Client Organizations in sustainable design; SCAD Alumni in the workforce; Future Generations represented in student worlds.
* **Interests:** Respectful representation of non-Western ontologies; ethical stewardship of cultural concepts; ensuring SCAD graduates possess genuine systems-thinking skills.
* **Risks:** Epistemic extraction and commercialization of sacred temporal models; diluting the meaning of deep-time philosophy into Silicon Valley marketing buzzwords.
* **Benefits:** Elevating Indigenous and deep-time frameworks to master-level design professionals who influence global infrastructure and media.
* **Concerns:** Cultural appropriation masked as "speculative worldmaking."
* **Unresolved Questions:** Did anyone invite living Indigenous knowledge holders into the curriculum design before applying AI to their philosophies?

### Position 4: People Excluded or Burdened (Those Bearing Hidden Labor or Externalities)
* **Primary Actors:**
  1. *Global South Data Workers:* Workers in Kenya, the Philippines, and elsewhere performing traumatic content filtering and dataset annotation.
  2. *Communities Hosting Hyperscale Data Centers:* Residents of regions experiencing water table depletion and fossil-fuel peaker plant pollution for AI cooling.
  3. *More-Than-Human Ecological Communities:* Rivers, migratory birds, soil biomes, and forests impacted by climate change acceleration from AI compute energy.
  4. *Unborn Seventh-Generation Humans (2176–2526 CE):* The descendants who will inherit the material and ideological fallout of the systems designed today.
* **Hidden Labor:** Uncompensated scraping of artist portfolios, climate scientists' public databases, and indigenous oral archives.
* **Access Needs:** Neurodivergent students requiring accessible multimodal inputs; students without high-end GPU hardware or subscription budgets.
* **Points of Refusal:**
  - Refusal to upload non-public community oral histories or sacred ceremonial structures into commercial LLM prompts.
  - Student right to opt out of generative tools without academic penalty, completing the course through analog speculative crafting.

## 2. Missing-Person Check (Audit Results)

**Audit Partner:** Team 06 (Material Ecology Studio)  
**Finding:**  
When reviewing our initial draft, Team 06 observed:
> *"Your stakeholder list is exclusively human, academic, and contemporary. You are teaching a course called 'Designing in Deep Time' that claims to center the Seventh Generation and non-human relations, yet your stakeholder snapshot completely omits non-human entities and the actual unborn humans of Year 2526."*

### Remediations Logged in Response to the Gap:
1. **Explicit Inclusion of the More-Than-Human:** We added the *Savannah River Basin* and *Coastal Georgia Salt Marsh Ecosystem* as explicit non-human stakeholders burdened by physical data center water consumption.
2. **Proxy Representation for the 7th Generation:** We instituted a mandatory **7th-Generation Proxy Role** in student design reviews: at least one peer reviewer must exclusively evaluate the project from the perspective of an inhabitant of 2226 CE.
3. **Point of Refusal Instituted:** We formally enacted a policy that students retain the explicit right to declare an **Analog Exemption**, choosing physical hand-drafted storyboards and physical material artifacts without grade disparity.
